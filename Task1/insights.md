# Task 1 - Insights & recommendations

Generated by `task1_analysis.py` from `insights_numbers.json`; do not edit by hand (edits are overwritten on the next run). Forecast test period: 2019-11-19 to 2020-08-07.

1. **The line is down about a fifth of the time.** 148 shutdown/idle events, 6,877 h (287 days, 21.8 % of the record). 72 are short stops under 2 h; 18 are outages over 48 h (longest 40.2 days) and drive most of the hours. 2019 was the worst year (2,939 h). **What counts as a shutdown:** the rule is median of the three drafts above -60 for 30+ min. That value sits in an almost empty valley (only 0.86 % of samples lie between -100 and -40, plots/05b). 87 of the 148 events keep inlet gas above 700 C for most of their length, so many are fan-off / idle stops with hot gas rather than cold shutdowns; the drafts collapsing to about 0 is the reason they count.
2. **Restarts are a distinct, unstable regime that is also over-represented before shutdowns.** The unstable state is 2.9 % of active time but 97 % of the first hour after a restart (partly by construction, since it is defined by high volatility) and 19.5 % of the 30 min before a shutdown (about 7x its normal share). Labels use trailing windows only. It is a moderate signal: about four in five pre-shutdown windows are in other states.
3. **Anomaly events are more common just before shutdowns than chance.** 7.4 % (40 of 544 in-operation events; 95 % interval 5.4-9.9 %) end within 1 h before a shutdown, against 0.59 % for a random operating timestamp: about 12.5x (interval 9.2-16.7x). Events are autocorrelated, so the interval is optimistic.
4. **Some anomalies are instrument faults.** Anomalies #1, #2 (material flatline for 200 min; material dropout for 165 min) show the material temperature leaving the process while gas temperatures and drafts stay normal: instrument faults, not process events. The "Degraded heat recovery (low outlet T)" state (4.4 % of active time) has outlet gas about 160 C below inlet and is worth checking for false air or an outlet thermocouple problem.
5. **Forecasting helps in steady operation, not at shutdown onset.** The 60-minute-ahead RMSE / MAE is 17.2 / 9.7 for gradient boosting against 21.4 / 12.4 for persistence. Averaged over all horizons from 5 to 60 min the figures are 15.3 / 8.9 against 19.4 / 11.3. A plain 6-step moving average already captures about 59 % of the boosting gain (about 49 % at 60 min), so most of the gain is smoothing sensor jitter. In windows that contain a shutdown start every model has RMSE 73-93.
6. **The process drifts, so models need monitoring.** Mean active inlet temperature is 886, 895, 889 and 902 C in 2017-2020, but its spread changes a lot (std 24, 34, 58, 18); inlet draft is stronger in 2020 (-207 vs -187 in 2018) and 2019 has the most downtime. On the test years the forecast error is similar (boosting RMSE 16.7 for the last six weeks of 2019, 15.0 for 2020), so I see no degradation on this single split. The choice of k = 5 holds across random seeds (adjusted Rand index 0.96) but not across time: models fitted on the first and second half agree only at ARI 0.44. That suggests refitting the states on a rolling window, monitoring per state, and retraining the forecaster on a schedule.
7. **Checks added after review.** (a) The 10th-90th percentile band from quantile gradient boosting covers 92 % of 60-minute-ahead test values overall against the 80 % intended (92 % in steady operation, so the band is wider than needed there, 51 C on average) but only 53 % in windows that contain a shutdown start (`forecast_intervals.csv`). The band does not warn about shutdowns. (b) State shares move a lot between years under the global model: the Normal state is 10 %, 48 %, 15 % and 80 % of active time in 2017-2020, and a model refitted on each year alone agrees with the global one only at ARI 0.09-0.47 (`state_stability_by_year.csv`). I did not test why; a level shift in inlet temperature is one candidate, since the states are partly defined by level. Compare state shares across years with care and refit on a rolling window (not done here). (c) A second detector, a state-conditional robust Mahalanobis distance (MinCovDet, chi-square 99 % cutoff), has at least half of the points beyond the cutoff in 100 % of the 544 in-operation events and in all 5 selected events, against 12 % for random windows of the same length and state. The events are unusual under a second, differently built method, but both detectors see the same features, so this is a consistency check and not independent confirmation (`anomaly_crosscheck_*.csv`).

## Recommendations
- **Early-warning rule (usable as a monitoring signal but not as a trip predictor).** Rule: inlet-draft rise above 40 toward zero together with an inlet-temperature fall above 30 C, both over 15 min, machine running. In a backtest on trailing windows it fires in the hour before 67 of 148 shutdowns (45.3 %), with a median lead time of 5 min, and 19.3 % of its 348 alerts are followed by a shutdown within an hour (1.14 false alerts per 100 operating hours). Use it as a dashboard flag combined with state, not as an automatic trip predictor.
- **Treat the restart hour separately**: suppress ordinary anomaly alerts in the first hour after restart and use ramp-rate checks instead.
- **Data-quality alerts**: flag more than 3 consecutive "Not Connect / I/O Timeout" samples and tags frozen or pinned at limits (-185, 1375, about 40 for material T).
- **Forecasting**: run forecasts only while the machine is active, add a shutdown-risk classifier, and add fan current / speed and feed rate, which could explain trips the temperatures cannot predict.
- **Validate hypotheses** against maintenance logs and operator notes before acting on any root cause.
